**Short answer:** if you left Typeform because of the 10-response free plan, move to
**Tally** (free, unlimited, the fastest editor there is). If you need the answers to be
good rather than just collected, use a conversational form that follows up:
**chatform** or **Jotform's AI Agents**. If everything already lives in Google, stay on
**Google Forms**. The rest of this post is the evidence for that, and the cases where
each of those picks is wrong.

A disclosure before anything else: I build chatform, which is one of the six. So every
number below comes from the vendor's own pricing or help pages, read in August and
September 2026, and each tool gets the section it deserves, including the ones that
beat us.

## Why people leave Typeform

It's rarely the design. Typeform still makes the best-looking one-question-per-screen
forms on the market, and if your form is a brand piece, that matters.

The reasons I hear are nearly always money-shaped. The free plan stops at **10
responses a month**, which is a demo, not a plan. The entry paid tier is $25 a month
billed yearly. And the thing that's genuinely new in this category (an AI that reads
the answer and asks a follow-up, or answers the respondent's question) isn't on
typeform.com at all. It's a separate product, Formless, starting at $59 a month for
250 AI conversations.

So the useful question isn't "what's like Typeform?" It's which of those three problems
you're trying to solve.

## 1. Tally: the best free form builder, full stop

If your complaint is the response cap, this is your answer. Tally's free plan has
unlimited forms and unlimited responses, and its editor (you type on a page, Notion
style, and blocks appear) is faster than anything else here, ours included.

**Choose Tally if** your form is mostly short, clear fields and you want it free
forever. It also has more native integrations than chatform: Notion, Airtable, Google
Sheets and Slack among them.

**Skip it if** the answers themselves are the problem. Tally doesn't follow up on a
vague answer, and its own help page says partial submissions don't trigger email
notifications, so the person who stops at question six is simply gone. We've written a
longer [Tally comparison](/tally-alternative).

## 2. Youform: a closer copy of Typeform, for less

Youform looks and behaves most like Typeform of anything on this list, one question
per screen, with unlimited responses on the free plan and a paid tier at $20 a month
billed yearly. It also verifies respondents by email or SMS, which is unusual.

**Choose Youform if** you like Typeform's format and just want to stop paying for it.

**Skip it if** you wanted something that does more than Typeform rather than the same
for less. There's no conversational AI, and I couldn't find anything on following up
with people who leave. More in [Youform vs chatform](/youform-alternative).

## 3. Fillout: the cheapest paid plan, and real abandonment emails

Fillout's free plan allows 1,000 responses a month and its paid plans start at $15 a
month billed yearly, the lowest on this list. It's strongest if your data lives in
Airtable or Notion, where its integrations go deeper than most.

It's also the only tool here besides chatform with built-in abandonment recovery: one
email, sent roughly half an hour after someone leaves. Their own guidance is that it
should only ever be sent once. That's a defensible position, and it's the opposite of
ours (more on that below).

**Choose Fillout if** you're on Airtable or Notion, or you want the cheapest paid tier.

**Skip it if** you need the form to hold a conversation. I couldn't find that
documented. [Fillout vs chatform](/fillout-alternative) has the full comparison.

## 4. Jotform: the closest thing to what we build

I'll be straight about this one. Jotform's AI Agents are a real conversational
form-filler with a knowledge base you train, and they start on the free plan with 100
conversations a month. If you want an AI that asks the questions and answers the
respondent's, Jotform does it.

Jotform is also simply much bigger: PDF generation, approvals, e-signatures, HIPAA
workflows, more than 40 payment gateways and tens of thousands of templates.

**Choose Jotform if** you need breadth, or you're already inside its ecosystem.

**Skip it if** 100 free responses a month is too few, or the $34 a month entry price
is more than a form is worth to you. And if the sprawl is the reason you're looking at
alternatives, you'll find it everywhere in the product. [Jotform vs chatform](/jotform-alternative)
goes line by line.

## 5. Google Forms: free, and already in everyone's account

Nothing beats Google Forms when the answers go straight into a spreadsheet and the
questions are obvious: an RSVP, a lunch order, a class register. It's free and
unlimited, and the Sheets output is instant and native, which we can't match (ours is a
spreadsheet feed you pull from).

**Choose Google Forms if** your organisation lives in Google Workspace and the questions
don't need interpreting.

**Skip it if** you need branding, anything beyond basic section logic, or answers that
come back better than what people first type. More in [Google Forms vs chatform](/google-forms-alternative).

## 6. chatform: when the answers have to be usable

This is ours, so weigh it accordingly.

chatform is a conversational form. It asks one question at a time like Typeform, but it
reads each answer. When someone types "growth" into "what do you want out of this?",
it asks which kind of growth. When they ask "is parking included?" halfway through, it
answers from the notes you gave it (knowledge entries are on Pro) and goes back to the question it was on. The answers
still come out as ordinary structured fields; a state machine owns the order, so the
model can't skip a required question or reorder the form (I've written about
[why the model never owns the conversation](/blog/the-model-never-owns-the-conversation)).

The free plan has unlimited responses (fair-use ceiling of 10,000 a month) on up to 100
forms, and 200 AI conversations. Paid plans start at $16 a month billed yearly.

Where it differs most is what happens after someone leaves. On Pro and above, chatform
sends up to three reminders, four hours, a day and three days apart, each worded
differently. Each one links back to the exact question they stopped on with their
answers still there. It can also hold a share of the people who left back and send them
nothing, so you can see whether the reminders did anything.

A real example: a university hackathon ran its registration on chatform this September.
129 people started, 55 finished. Of the 98 who left partway and got a reminder, 24
finished afterwards. I won't call those 24 "recovered". That form ran without a
holdout, and some of those students were always coming back before the deadline. But
it's the kind of number you'll never see from a tool that sends nothing.

**Choose chatform if** a one-word answer is a failure for you: client intake, job
applications, quote requests, feedback you actually want to act on.

**Skip it if** you need video questions, payment processing or a large one-click
integration catalogue. We have none of those. Typeform, Jotform and Tally all do
better there.

## So which one?

- **You only hit the response cap:** Tally.
- **You like Typeform's format and want it cheaper:** Youform.
- **You run on Airtable or Notion:** Fillout.
- **You need everything, including payments and PDFs:** Jotform.
- **Your team lives in Google Workspace:** Google Forms.
- **You need better answers, not just more of them:** chatform, or Jotform's AI Agents.
